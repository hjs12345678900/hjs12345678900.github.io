01 PhD research

Supermassive
black-hole evolution.

How do black holes gain their mass, how does that growth connect to galaxy assembly, and which observables still remember the answer?

The question

Connecting individual accretion episodes to a cosmic population.

My PhD research develops interpretable models for the evolving black-hole mass function. The central challenge is to connect short, variable accretion episodes and galaxy mergers to the population of black holes observed across redshift—without losing sight of the assumptions that drive the result.

01.A

Population evolution

Evolving black-hole mass distributions through cosmic time and testing which growth histories can reproduce the observed population.

01.B

Accretion physics

Exploring duty cycles, Eddington-ratio distributions, radiative efficiency, and light-curve prescriptions as explicit model ingredients.

01.C

Merger connection

Conditioning black-hole growth on galaxy assembly and asking where merger-triggered activity leaves a measurable signature.

Working method

Equations → code → experiments → interpretable figures.

  1. FrameWrite the physical assumptions and observables explicitly.
  2. ModelBuild reproducible numerical experiments and controlled variants.
  3. CompareConfront the resulting population with empirical constraints.
  4. InterpretSeparate robust conclusions from parameter-dependent behaviour.
